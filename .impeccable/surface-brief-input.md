# Surface Brief: web/index.html

## Scope and mode
Scope: Single-page responsive workbench
Mode: Operate

## Audience and task
Audience: AI & systems engineers evaluating foundation models on Terminal-Bench 4.0.
Task: Answer the single engineering decision: "When per-task cost does not exceed $X, what is the highest score Terminal-Bench 4.0 can buy?"

## Direction contract

### THESIS
A high-density astrometric observatory workbench: treating model benchmark evaluation as a Hertzsprung–Russell diagram of intelligence, where the Pareto frontier forms the luminous Main Sequence. Refuses the generic SaaS-card template, floating soft shadows, and decorative dashboard widgets.

### OWN-WORLD
Deep observatory space palette (Dark #070b14, #0d1527, #38bdf8, #f59e0b; Light #f8fafc, #ffffff, #0284c7, #d97706). Precise astronomical coordinate lines, high-clarity tabular numbers, spectral emission colors for model vendors, and zero decorative fluff.

### STORY
The engineer arrives with a budget limit per task. The budget ladder immediately delivers the conclusive answer (best model and score under <=$5, <=$10, <=$15). The interactive scatter plot plots all candidates with clear model names, tier labels, and confidence intervals, while the stepped Main Sequence proves optimality.

### FIRST VIEWPORT
Top row: Factual title, data source switch, theme toggle, telemetry facts, and live sync button. Primary evidence: Three budget ladder cards (<=$5, <=$10, <=$15) anchored alongside the coordinate scatter plot. On mobile, budget tiers stack above the chart, and the chart container opens scrolled directly to the low-cost / frontier edge.
